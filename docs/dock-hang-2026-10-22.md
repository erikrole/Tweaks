# CalDigit dock incident and network-context hotfix — 2026-10-22

The user reports that connecting the CalDigit TS5 Plus froze the menu-bar clock
and required a forced reboot. They confirmed the 09:18 reboot, before the final
0.1.6 install. The incident therefore occurred with 0.1.5 build 8. The CalDigit
remains connected during this investigation; no dock reconnect, intentional
interface toggle, share eject, credential change or firmware change was performed.
A later user-authorized single iPhone attachment is recorded separately below.

## Evidence

- At 09:14:43–09:14:49, the prior boot recorded repeated display reconfiguration.
  WindowServer logged synchronization timeouts at 09:14:49 and a fence timeout at
  09:15:16. These do not identify which component caused the display stall.
- At 09:15:03, the installed Tweaks 0.1.5 build 8 crashed with
  `EXC_BREAKPOINT / SIGTRAP`. Thread 5 was `SCDynamicStore notifications`.
  The matching UUID in the retained 0.1.5 dSYM symbolized the app frame to
  `@objc closure #2 in ShareConnectivityWatcher.start()`, line 466: the context
  release callback. Its stack was `__SCDynamicStoreDeallocate` → the app callback
  → Swift executor check → libdispatch queue assertion.
- The main thread was in the normal AppKit event loop at the crash. This proves
  a Tweaks callback defect, not that Tweaks caused the preceding WindowServer
  stall or the entire machine freeze. No matching kernel panic report or retained
  pre-reboot kernel records were available in the inspected diagnostics.
- The same lifetime callbacks were present in 0.1.6. Its prior cancellation/status
  fixes did not repair this defect.

Raw diagnostics and preference snapshots are private ignored files under
`.build/dock-hang-20261022/`; they are not release assets or repository content.

revision and dock socket were not physically inspected. Conflicting reseller

Dell 3840 × 1600 at 60 Hz and LG 2560 × 2880 at 60 Hz, extended. Dock firmware

Neither those recipients nor the LG cable can be assigned responsibility from

single causal chain. In particular, the Astra comparison below weakens the fence
warning as evidence of a persistent graphics freeze. Tweaks cannot sustain a
running callback after it dies, but its earlier work or OS cleanup can still be
part of the trigger; the app is not exonerated by the later timestamps.

Symbolizing the graphics warning sites resolves to
`CA::Render::Context::check_sync_reqs(double)` and

sites, not blocked-thread stacks or proof of which client owns the context.
WindowServer emits records through 09:15:53; other processes continue through
09:16:29. Background activity is compatible with the reported frozen graphical
session and does not establish whether the kernel hung later. No retained kernel
entries, matching panic, explicit GPU/DCP panic or event-tap timeout was found in
this window. Missing retained evidence cannot rule those causes out.

Three-second current samples show Tweaks and Control Center main threads idle in
their run loops. Current WindowServer sampling is protected and was unavailable
without administrator authentication. No password prompt was requested: a healthy
current stack cannot reconstruct the earlier stall.

## Independent Astra diagnosis

At the user's request, GPT-6 Astra independently reviewed the retained logs and
source without reconnecting devices, changing app lifecycle/settings or taking
new live samples. Its comparison found five further WindowServer fence timeouts
in the usable post-reboot session: 09:41:11.433, 09:43:22.970, 09:54:58.889,
09:57:42.187 and 10:00:23.087 CDT. That session also has a synchronize timeout
at 09:18:59.814 and thousands of the same `Unsupported image function XRGB`
warnings. These messages are not unique signatures of the reported full freeze;
their recurrence does not prove that every instance is harmless.

The earlier file named `prior-boot.json` contains both boots: 1,283 incident-boot
records and 74,190 current-boot records. The expanded locally filtered incident
dataset remains correct: all 292,469 records belong to the incident boot. The
comparison and source-boundary checks were independently reproduced before this
report correction. Private derived aggregates remain outside version control.

WindowServer's event-processing thread continues handling window/client activity
after the 09:14:49 warnings and the Tweaks crash, with its last retained record at
09:15:14.960. Other WindowServer threads continue later. Thus the early warnings
do not establish that the entire WindowServer was already frozen at 09:14:49.
The last retained log line is not a blocked-thread stack or a measured freeze onset.

At 09:15:03.124, WindowServer closes Tweaks' connection with eight windows and
eight CoreAnimation contexts. MenuBarAgent then invalidates Tweaks' status-item
scenes; its final retained record is at 09:15:03.132. ControlCenter's last retained
record is at 09:15:07.625. Source confirms an ordinary native `NSStatusItem`, with
no custom menu-host protocol implicated. This is consistent with normal crash
cleanup. An OS failure during that teardown remains possible, but the logs do not
prove it, nor do they connect it to the Bluetooth watchdog.

The defensible provisional sequence is dock wake, display/network reconfiguration,
the confirmed Tweaks release-callback crash, and graphical disruption whose
mechanism remains unknown. Normal share-restoration completion can stop its
connectivity watcher in 0.1.5; the release trap does not itself establish hung
network IPC. The evidence cannot choose between a dock/display problem, an OS
scene/compositor problem exposed by app death, or multiple concurrent faults.
The user subsequently confirmed that the pointer moved while apps did not respond.
They did not try or do not remember whether Command-Tab or typing produced visible
changes. Astra therefore ranks a shared graphical-session/input-processing or
presentation stall as the strongest symptom fit, with moderate confidence;
a menu-bar-only failure is unlikely to explain the whole report. Pointer movement
shows that some input and visible updating survived. It does not establish normal
app event delivery or window presentation, and the retained evidence cannot
distinguish ignored input from app updates that never became visible.

The left-button event tap described below is compatible with pointer motion and
failed clicks, making it a relevant hardening candidate. It does not explain the
frozen clock by itself. Tweaks' main thread was idle at its crash; WindowServer
later closes its connection and removes input-deferring rules. A continuously
running Tweaks callback cannot sustain failure after app death, although an earlier
trigger or downstream OS fault remains possible. No incident fix follows from
this symptom alone, and no app code, feature switches or shortcuts were changed.
Further healthy-session samples cannot reconstruct the missing historical stacks
or keyboard behavior. No additional recall question is pending.

## Recurring history and the VPN/directory lead

The user reports that the syndrome has occurred for months, before the TS5 Plus,
and only while GlobalProtect is connected or reconnecting. Connecting an iOS
device can also trigger it intermittently, through a dock or a MacBook port.
The TS5 cannot be a necessary original cause. A direct-port iOS trigger establishes
a different immediate attachment event, not that all docks/external displays were
absent. Similar symptoms do not establish one mechanism across all episodes.
The retained Tweaks repository lineage starts October 11; recent implementations
cannot explain a months-long origin, although the October 22 crash can still
contribute to that episode. Complete earlier software/hardware history is unknown.

The strongest concrete older failure is an October 20 11:58:29–11:58:35 CDT
`opendirectoryd` service-process watchdog stackshot. It records 513 threads and an
explicit 512 dispatch-thread hard limit, with synchronous operations blocked.
505 shown threads serve PID-specific requests. Matching the SystemCache module's
arm64e UUID permits symbolication to membership/cache lookup functions including
`mbr_entry_search_online`, `mbr_cache_fetch_entry`, `process_getpwuid` and kernel
authorization processing. Queued requests are associated with multiple processes,
including filesystem services, ControlCenter and Finder. This does not identify
which client caused the blockage or prove their main threads were all blocked.
WindowServer was executing rendering work in that older snapshot. Thus directory
exhaustion is established, but its connection to the reported graphical freeze is
not. The October 21 WindowServer report is a CPU-resource report, not a freeze stack.

October 22 includes physical-interface/tunnel and resolver changes, followed by
directory-domain/catalog connection failures and LDAP resets scoped to `utun0`.
These are real errors, but a usable post-reboot startup provides counterexamples:

| Retained signature | Incident | Usable startup |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| DNS upstream deadline records | 224 | 55 |
| DNS forwarding server-failure records | 20 | 24 |
| Directory trust/node connection-failure records | 7 | 60 |
| LDAP 389/3268 failed-flow records | 2 | 7 |
| Failed SMB authentication/open followed by NetAuth success | 1 | 1 |

These are positive counterexamples, not comparable failure rates. The startup
control differs from a matched wake/handoff. Its query hit a 25-second limit;
53,931 complete retained records were explicitly filtered locally to the current
boot and 09:18:00–09:20:30. Raw output exceeded the requested end, so relying on
CLI bounds alone would be incorrect. Interrupted enumeration cannot establish
completeness or absence. The usable session has authentication failure at
09:19:08.417 followed by success at 09:19:09.552. In the incident, failure at
09:15:02.019 is followed by NTLM fallback and NetAuth status 0 at 09:15:03.120;
watcher cancellation and the app's release-callback trap immediately follow.
That specific SMB restoration completed rather than remaining hung.

Astra's leading hypothesis is attachment/network activity interacting with
VPN-dependent directory/identity services, potentially exposing service contention
or exhaustion. It is not a demonstrated GlobalProtect tunnel deadlock, routing
loop or vendor-specific defect. DNS disruption, tunnel coexistence, directory
behavior and SystemCache remain candidates. Apple's documentation confirms the
DNS/Kerberos/LDAP dependencies of directory integration; Tailscale documents that
coexistence depends on software and configuration. Neither source establishes
this incident's cause.
[Apple directory integration](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/deployment/depd1a7cad1f/1/web/1.0),
[Tailscale interoperability](https://tailscale.com/docs/reference/interoperability).

## App paths still worth hardening

Read-only source review found synchronous global input/display paths that deserve
separate work, but no incident evidence establishes them as the cause:

- Green-button handling uses a session event tap on main, synchronous window-list
  discovery and AX calls before returning a click. Per-call AX timeouts do not
  bound the entire callback, and timeout disablement immediately re-enables it.
- Away dimming writes built-in brightness and external gamma after screen changes
  while dimmed, and restores ColorSync synchronously. Normal resume resets the
  two-minute idle threshold. The fresh Away assertion does not establish that
  dimming ran during the six seconds before the synchronize timeouts.
- CoreAudio discovery/listener work on main can stall Tweaks if driver IPC stalls.
  No blocked driver stack was retained here.

Automatic window layouts were disabled in the captured preferences. No global
display capture, display-configuration transaction, or registered CoreGraphics
display-reconfiguration callback was found in Tweaks. The existing main-thread
run-loop crash stack cannot prove that earlier input/display work never blocked.

## Repair

0.1.7 build 10 moves the context retain/release functions outside `MainActor`.
SystemConfiguration's selected notification queue does not constrain the queue
used to destroy its context. A nonisolated immutable Sendable box now owns a
main-actor callback that captures the watcher weakly. Notifications hop to
MainActor, and session-generation checks reject delivery after stop/restart.
NWPath notifications use the same generation boundary. Existing saved preferences,
share choices, pause state, and shortcut assignments are not migrated or changed.

Regressions invoke the actual C context lifetime pointers on a background task,
verify balanced box deallocation, and verify background notifications deliver on
the main thread without retaining the watcher. They do not subscribe to live
network notifications or change network configuration.
